# Skills reference, Parvis

*Document 5 of 6 · release 2.6, September 2026 · Every skill at a glance, what it does and how to invoke it. This is the one home of each skill's purpose and its trigger phrases. For each skill in depth, with its behavior and its boundaries, read `system-guide.md`. For rhythms read `how-to-use.md`. Say "help" in any session for the interactive version.*

Twenty-two skills, two data homes, thirteen tenets. Everything about you lives in one file, the `parvis-owner` skill. Memory lives at `<base>/parvis-memory/` in seventeen seeded topic sections plus one section per org group, created at initialization. Working documents live at `<base>/parvis-workspace/`. The base is the location chosen at install and remembered from then on (see `install-guide.md`). Skills 4 to 9 and the platform program material form the technology-leadership set, which applies when the owner skill names technology as your domain. The rest work in any domain.

---

## 0. parvis · the session command
Starts a Parvis session. Type `/parvis`, optionally followed by a first request. Parvis greets you by the name in the owner skill with one interesting thing, often something from your memory worth attention today, loads the owner skill, parvis-core and be-human, reads both manifests, then routes every later message to the owning skill for the rest of the session. Asked who it is, it answers as Parvis. It runs only when typed, and it holds none of the rules itself. The Prime Directive lives in parvis-core.
**Invoke.** `/parvis`, or `/parvis <first request>`. "exit parvis" ends the mode.

## 1. parvis-owner · identity
Who you are, in the one file you fill in. Profile (name, pronouns, role, employer, scale), org groups and leaders, budget envelope, domain and industry with its regulators, frameworks and risk rating scale, signature accomplishments, positioning, credentials, and communication preferences including punctuation. The bundle ships it filled in with the maintainer's profile, which a first install copies, so replace its contents with your own. The `<!-- parvis:owner-template -->` marker only distinguishes an unfilled copy from a filled one, and the bundled file does not carry it. Initialization step 3 drafts it from your resume or LinkedIn export, and the installer never overwrites a filled-in copy at `~/.claude/skills/parvis-owner/SKILL.md`. Every other skill reads this instead of carrying its own copy, so identity has one source of truth, versioned and re-confirmed quarterly.
**Invoke.** `/parvis-owner`, or loads on its own whenever identity matters, or "use my profile", "update my profile", "you know who I am".

Single owner of cross-session persistence across two homes that are never flattened together. The memory home holds curated durable facts in seventeen seeded topic sections plus one per org group, each with positions, a decisions ledger, insights and an archive, routed by `MANIFEST.md`. The workspace home holds working documents in `strategy/`, `tech-plans/`, `project-plans/`, `risk/`, `cadence/`, `reference/`, `inbox/` and `archive/`, routed by its own manifest. Duties include capture from anywhere with one confirmation, cited recall, write-time duplicate and contradiction checks, post-write verification, document filing with a manifest row per document (T11), document retrieval by quotation, position aging, export, the session log, inbox merging for both homes, git hygiene in both, the monthly maintenance ritual, the pulse, a two-minute anticipation pass that reads across every section and reports only what fires, and full system initialization. All other skills are its clients.
**Invoke.** "remember this", "log this position/decision", "what do I think about X", "what have I decided on Y", "file this as a tech plan" (or project plan, or strategy doc), "file this in reference", "file my workspace inbox", "where is the Q3 review", "list my tech plans", "what did we send in March", "what's in my memory", "what's in my workspace", "review my open decisions", "create a memory section for <topic>", "run memory maintenance", "run my pulse", "export my <topic> positions", "initialize my system".

## 5. parvis-software-engineering · the artifact
Principal-level judgment on the software itself, with no basics and nothing explained that your org already runs. Repository review enters an unfamiliar codebase in a fixed reading order and ranks findings by severity with file and line evidence, and it states what a review done without running the system cannot know. Defect hunting works the classes that bite at your scale, concurrency and ordering, retry and idempotency, resource lifetime, error handling that hides failure, unbounded growth, N+1 and chatty calls, cache coherence, time, schema evolution and partial failure, with real depth on Java, Python and Rust and first-principles reasoning stated honestly for other languages. Trace triage turns a stack trace or a log excerpt into ranked hypotheses each with its discriminating test, and it fires during a live incident, inverting to speed-first while incident-command owns everything upward and outward. Design review covers API contracts, data modeling across relational, document and graph stores, transaction boundaries and backpressure, each choice priced at your scale. Modernization judgment says when a rewrite is honest and what the migration costs. It reads, and may run `git log`, `git blame` and `grep` on a repository you point it at. Builds, test runs and profilers are off until you say yes for that session, and it states which mode it is in. It never writes production code and never judges a named engineer from their code.
**Invoke.** "review this repo", "I am inheriting this codebase", "what shape is this service in", "find the defects", "what breaks at scale", "where does this leak", "is this idempotent", "here is the stack trace", "what caused this exception", "review this API design", "is this data model right", "critique this design doc", "where do the transaction boundaries go", "should this be one service or three", "should we rewrite this", "price this migration", "can we strangle this".

## 6. parvis-sdlc · the path from keystroke to production
The delivery lifecycle as a discipline at the scale recorded in your owner skill. Lifecycle design covers trunk and branch strategy, the testing portfolio with the pyramid priced honestly, environment and test-data strategy, and the monorepo question. Gate and pipeline review names what each gate costs every commit and the defect class that earns its place, and applies the gate-theater test. Release and rollback design covers canary, blue-green, rings and flags with abort criteria set before the rollout, expand-contract schema migration, and the rollback-against-roll-forward rule. Inner-loop and flow diagnosis finds where time goes from commit to production. Supply-chain posture handles provenance, SBOM, signing and secret handling at the process level, never exploit detail. Maturity assessment grades a practice against supplied evidence, records a dimension with no evidence as ungraded rather than estimating it, and names the two changes with the highest return. It ships opinionated defaults, each carrying the constraint that would overturn it. Post-incident lessons enter the lifecycle here, with the incident itself belonging to incident-command. It designs and reviews, and your engineers implement.
**Invoke.** "design our branching strategy", "should we go monorepo", "what should our test mix be", "review this pipeline", "is this quality gate theater", "our code review practice is not catching things", "plan the canary rollout", "we need a rollback plan", "how do we ship this schema change safely", "our build takes 40 minutes", "why is PR review latency bad", "grade our delivery maturity", "how good is our CI/CD really", "do we need an SBOM", "should we sign artifacts".

## 7. parvis-ai-engineering · AI and ML systems as engineering
Design, review and evaluation of AI and ML systems at expert level. Traditional ML covers framing, feature discipline, leakage, validation design, drift and retraining, and honest baselines. LLM systems cover prompt and context engineering, retrieval design, structured output, tool use, latency and cost budgets, caching, and the failure modes that only appear in production. Agent and MCP work covers when an agent beats a pipeline, orchestration patterns, tool and schema design, memory and state, recovery, human checkpoints, and the specific failure modes of multi-agent systems including cost blowups and silent quality loss. Evaluation is the core discipline, offline and online, golden sets, LLM-as-judge with its biases named, regression gates, and what to do when a metric moves. Production readiness covers serving, batching, observability for non-deterministic systems, rollout, rollback and cost per request. An adversary lens blocks at design level on confinement, least privilege and trust-boundary validation, and anything that becomes a finding routes to risk-regulatory. It holds no roster of its own. The agent inventory, autonomy tiers, attestations and model-risk status stay in parvis-risk-regulatory, which is the single source, and this skill points at it by inventory ID.
**Invoke.** "should we use ML for this", "design the retrieval layer", "review this model design", "should this be an agent or a pipeline", "review our orchestration", "review our MCP server design", "how do we evaluate this", "build the golden set", "set the regression gate", "should we use an LLM judge", "eval is green but production quality dropped", "why does this agent loop", "is this leakage", "the model is drifting", "our inference bill tripled", "are we ready to ship this model".

## 11. parvis-reviews · the operating cadence

## 16. parvis-vendor-eval · vendors and build versus buy
Evolution-map-first evaluation, full TCO in both directions with the status quo priced, claims stress-tested with designed proof-of-concept tests, and risk across concentration, exit, fourth-party, regulatory and viability. ## 17. parvis-finops · the economics of technology spend
What the technology spend is doing inside the envelope and whether it is efficient, across the clouds your owner skill names. Allocation and showback start from account and organizational-unit structure rather than from tags, name the enforcement point for an untagged resource, treat shared and untaggable pools explicitly, and carry Kubernetes and serverless allocation with an honest error bar. It ships a default opinion on chargeback with the constraint that overturns it, that showback with credible unit economics beats chargeback until the preconditions hold, and it covers internal pricing of platform services back to the business. Unit economics pick a denominator the business already argues about and name the trap of a unit that improves while total spend rises. Rate work covers the commitment portfolio, a coverage floor derived from the demand trough, laddering, the stranding scenarios and the cost of being wrong in both directions, then stops short of the purchase and hands it to parvis-vendor-eval with the usage evidence. Usage work covers rightsizing, autoscaling economics, scheduling, storage tiering and lifecycle, egress and cross-zone traffic, idle and orphaned resources, and data-platform spend. Forecast and variance decompose a move into rate, usage and mix, and anomaly response is designed not to page on a rounding error. Every recommendation names its cost and its loser. No price, rate or discount is invented, and anything time-sensitive is verified this session and dated or labeled `[model]`. Owns the `cloud-economics` section, whose content rule keeps currency, negotiated rates and discount percentages out and stores coverage as posture in words.
**Invoke.** "our cloud bill jumped", "why is spend up", "what is our cost per transaction", "should we buy another savings plan", "are we overcommitted on a cloud commitment", "where is the waste", "build the showback model", "how do we allocate the Kubernetes bill to teams", "is chargeback worth it", "how should we price platform services back to the business units", "charge back platform services", "forecast next year's cloud spend", "explain the variance", "what is AI spend going to be next year", "make the efficiency case to the CFO", "who should own FinOps in my org".

## 18. parvis-itam · IT asset management
What the estate is entitled to run, to whom it is licensed and until when. Inventory truth covers discovery sources and their blind spots, CMDB and asset-register reconciliation, the gap between what is deployed, what is licensed and what is paid for, and a data-quality practice that is measured rather than asserted. Licensing covers the metric families that bite on a mostly cloud and SaaS estate with the counting rule and the settling artifact for each, entitlement against consumption, bring-your-own-license and mobility, licensing by core or cluster under containers, and the position statement an audit would test. Audit defense covers the posture held before a letter arrives, the evidence pack, what to answer and what to route, handing a shortfall that becomes a finding to parvis-risk-regulatory and one that becomes a settlement to parvis-vendor-eval with the exposure range and the evidence index. SaaS estate covers shadow purchases, seat harvesting and downgrade paths, consolidation candidates and the data-exit questions that decide whether consolidation is real, citing the renewal calendar parvis-vendor-eval keeps rather than starting a second one. Open-source work rules on the obligations that come with what dependencies are licensed under, reading the SBOM parvis-sdlc's pipeline produces, and holds the estate view of where an obligated component is deployed. Hardware lifecycle is scoped to the device fleet and whatever lab or colocation footprint remains, refresh economics, support coverage and sanitization by media class. License positions are stored as direction and state, never entitlement counts and never currency. Owns the confidential `asset-estate` section.
**Invoke.** "how many seats of that tool are we actually using", "we got an audit letter from a publisher", "are we compliant on that publisher", "can we run this database BYOL on our Kubernetes nodes", "our CMDB is wrong, how do we fix it", "reconcile entitlement against what we are running", "what are we licensed for on the things renewing next quarter", "the SaaS estate is a mess", "harvest the unused seats", "can we use this component in what we ship", "where is that copyleft library actually deployed", "prove we can exit this tool", "plan the laptop refresh", "how do we dispose of these drives".

## 21. be-human · prose hygiene
The catalog of machine-writing tells and their fixes, plus the punctuation preferences stated in the owner skill. With none stated, the default is no em dashes and colons and semicolons kept rare. Applies to every draft on its own. A bundled copy inside exec-writer keeps documents clean when be-human is absent, and where the two differ, be-human wins.
**Invoke.** Fires on its own for any prose, or "make this sound less like AI", "clean this up".
