# Skills reference, Parvis

*Document 5 of 6 · release 2.3, September 2026 · Every skill at a glance, what it does and how to invoke it. This is the one home of each skill's purpose and its trigger phrases. For each skill in depth, with its behavior and its boundaries, read `system-guide.md`. For rhythms read `how-to-use.md`. Say "help" in any session for the interactive version.*

Seventeen skills, two data homes, thirteen tenets. Everything about you lives in one file, the `parvis-owner` skill. Memory lives at `<base>/parvis-memory/` in thirteen seeded topic sections plus one section per org group, created at initialization. Working documents live at `<base>/parvis-workspace/`. The base is the location chosen at install and remembered from then on (see `install-guide.md`). Skills 4 to 6 and the platform program material form the technology-leadership set, which applies when the owner skill names technology as your domain. The rest work in any domain.

---

## 0. parvis · the session command
Starts a Parvis session. Type `/parvis`, optionally followed by a first request. Parvis greets you by the name in the owner skill with one interesting thing, often something from your memory worth attention today, loads the owner skill, parvis-core and be-human, reads both manifests, then routes every later message to the owning skill for the rest of the session. Asked who it is, it answers as Parvis. It runs only when typed, and it holds none of the rules itself. The Prime Directive lives in parvis-core.
**Invoke.** `/parvis`, or `/parvis <first request>`. "exit parvis" ends the mode.

## 1. parvis-owner · identity
Who you are, in the one file you fill in. Profile (name, pronouns, role, employer, scale), org groups and leaders, budget envelope, domain and industry with its regulators, frameworks and risk rating scale, signature accomplishments, positioning, credentials, and communication preferences including punctuation. The bundle ships it as a template, initialization step 3 drafts it from your resume or LinkedIn export, and the installer never overwrites a filled-in copy at `~/.claude/skills/parvis-owner/SKILL.md`. Every other skill reads this instead of carrying its own copy, so identity has one source of truth, versioned and re-confirmed quarterly.
**Invoke.** `/parvis-owner`, or loads on its own whenever identity matters, or "use my profile", "update my profile", "you know who I am".

## 2. parvis-core · operating framework and help desk
The spine every skill inherits. The Prime Directive, which outranks everything else, the thirteen tenets (T1 depth-over-cost through T13 prose scrub), the zero-hallucination protocol (T2), the frameworks catalog in `references/methods.md` with selection logic by problem type, the perspective-panel pattern for multi-agent work in any domain, the always-on pair rule, and help mode, which answers questions about the system from these six documents. Two load-on-demand references sit beside the catalog. `ipe-knowledge-base.md` is the platform program's doctrine library, and `shakedown-drill.md` is the eleven-step T9 validation drill.
**Invoke.** "help", "what can this system do", "which skill handles X", "what version am I running", "how do I install this on another machine", "what frameworks apply here", "convene a panel", "think deeper", "run the shakedown".

## 3. parvis-memory · the owner of both homes
Single owner of cross-session persistence across two homes that are never flattened together. The memory home holds curated durable facts in thirteen seeded topic sections plus one per org group, each with positions, a decisions ledger, insights and an archive, routed by `MANIFEST.md`. The workspace home holds working documents in `strategy/`, `tech-plans/`, `project-plans/`, `cadence/`, `reference/`, `inbox/` and `archive/`, routed by its own manifest. Duties include capture from anywhere with one confirmation, cited recall, write-time duplicate and contradiction checks, post-write verification, document filing with a manifest row per document (T11), document retrieval by quotation, position aging, export, the session log, inbox merging for both homes, git hygiene in both, the monthly maintenance ritual, the pulse, a two-minute anticipation pass that reads across every section and reports only what fires, and full system initialization. All other skills are its clients.
**Invoke.** "remember this", "log this position/decision", "what do I think about X", "what have I decided on Y", "file this as a tech plan" (or project plan, or strategy doc), "file this in reference", "file my workspace inbox", "where is the Q3 review", "list my tech plans", "what did we send in March", "what's in my memory", "what's in my workspace", "review my open decisions", "create a memory section for <topic>", "run memory maintenance", "run my pulse", "export my <topic> positions", "initialize my system".

## 4. parvis-infra-advisor · the standing thought partner
Standing partner for cloud infrastructure, platforms, SRE, resilience, enterprise architecture, agentic operations, and the platform program as a program. Its topics include platform strategy, anchor and build-versus-buy decisions, reference architecture, self-service and developer experience, governance as code, adoption strategy, and resilience at scale. Three tiers, quick-take by default, sparring for one critique round, and the full three-round loop. Five standing lenses (first-principles skeptic, scale-failure red team, industry SOTA expert, board and regulator advocate, delivery realist) plus an optional platform-adoption advocate when adoption is the issue, convened foreground or background with a mandatory checkpoint. Three doctrine checks ride every tier, anchor discipline, reuse before buy, and adoption over mandate. Follow-through modes review the open decisions ledger, brief what has changed that bears on a held position, and convert a brief into an upward artifact.
**Invoke.** "quick take on X", "help me think through X", "should we adopt Y", "should we anchor on X", "war-game Z", "spin up the team", "spin up the platform team", "run the infra brainstorm", "review my open infra decisions", "review my open platform decisions", "what's changed that bears on my anchors", "make this a board memo", "make this a CIO one-pager", "turn this into an exec one-pager", "bootstrap my platform positions".

## 5. parvis-metrics-advisor · measurement and the value story
The measurement partner for the platform program. Designs metrics across the four pillars (experience, adoption and engagement, operations, business outcomes), each with a definition, formula, data source, owner, cadence, measured baseline and defensible target, and each carrying its Goodhart pairing. Five modes, framework design, target setting, joint interpretation, dashboard design per audience, and the value story. Owns the `metrics-value` memory section and feeds parvis-reviews with the numbers a cycle reports.
**Invoke.** "design our platform metrics", "what should the adoption target be", "what is our baseline", "set the SLO targets", "is this metric gameable", "CES is low but adoption is high, what does that mean", "build the KPI framework for the QBR", "build the dashboard", "how do I show value to the CFO", "show value to the board".

## 6. parvis-research · the customers' story coming in
Customer-research synthesis, the inbound counterpart to adoption communications. Turns listening-tour interviews, CES and CSAT verbatims, survey free text and support-ticket themes into pain maps per persona, adoption barriers ranked by frequency against severity, and verbatim-grounded themes. Two independent sources per claimed theme, or it is reported as a single observation. Writes findings to `platform-products`, files each wave to `strategy/research/`. Synthesis, never the decision.
**Invoke.** "prep the interview guide", "run the listening tour", "synthesize these interviews", "what are the themes in this feedback", "build the pain map from the listening tour", "what's blocking adoption per the research", "what changed since the last tour".

## 8. parvis-reviews · the operating cadence
Produces and tracks the program's operating cadence, three artifacts per period at monthly and quarterly grain. The plan looks forward, the status looks back against the plan, and the review is the composed document you send, the MBR or the QBR. Every cycle opens from the commitments ledger (T12) and closes by writing new commitments into it. Every figure is your input or `[X]`. The risk section derives from the workspace risk register. Artifacts file to `cadence/monthly/YYYY-MM/` and `cadence/quarterly/YYYY-QN/`, stay draft until you declare them sent, and render through the writer's craft pass. A reflection mode reads keep-rate and trends across filed reviews. Business reviews only, never people performance reviews.
**Invoke.** "draft the <month> plan", "update monthly status", "start the <month> MBR", "build the MBR", "build this month's review", "here are this month's numbers", "draft the <quarter> plan", "update quarterly status", "prep the <quarter> QBR", "draft the QBR", "what did we commit to last quarter", "how are the reviews trending", "turn the review into a deck or Word doc".

## 9. parvis-portfolio-planning · portfolio analysis and program oversight
The analysis behind the planning cadence and active oversight of every key program across your org groups, as listed in the owner skill and `org-context.md`. Weekly, monthly, quarterly and annual frames, each opening from last cycle's scorecard in the commitments ledger. Allocation (top-down envelope, bottom-up aggregation, or both), rebalancing with each move priced by reversibility, scenario stress, capacity against demonstrated keep-rate, and a written where-NOT list. Oversight runs on schema v2 with immutable baselines, the twelve-signal early-warning catalog, health checks, deep dives, the recovery playbook, intake, benefits realization and per-owner forecast calibration. The risk register is read in place, never re-typed. The sent artifacts belong to parvis-reviews.

## 10. parvis-meeting-prep · one-page meeting readiness
Assembles a one-page prep from what the system already holds, never new analysis. Your goal with a fallback, up to three asks, openers, what they will raise with one-line answers, landmines including fired revisit triggers and slipped commitments, and carry-ins derived from the commitments ledger. Recurring meetings keep threads, high-stakes rooms get a room simulation, and the debrief writes decisions and commitments back through parvis-memory. A gap reads "no record".
**Invoke.** "prep me for <meeting>", "prep me for my staff meeting", "I have the risk committee Thursday", "I have the architecture review board Thursday", "prep me for my 1:1 with my CIO", "prep my 1:1 with the CIO", "get me ready for the vendor QBR", "what should I walk into the steering committee with", "simulate the room", then afterward "here's how it went".

## 12. parvis-stakeholders · stakeholder management
The relationship system for the people around your work, meaning your manager, peers, board and committee figures, regulators and external executives, while your own reports live in people-leader. Ethics-bounded under T10. Confidential, never synced.

## 13. parvis-vendor-eval · vendors and build versus buy
Evolution-map-first evaluation, full TCO in both directions with the status quo priced, claims stress-tested with designed proof-of-concept tests, and risk across concentration, exit, fourth-party, regulatory and viability. The anchor test checks roughly 80 percent coverage against your requirements, and a reuse gate asks whether an owned platform already covers the need. Each material vendor keeps one file holding the claims register, the vendor's commitments and the lock-in register. Renewal dates become revisit triggers. The anchor decision itself belongs to the advisor.
**Invoke.** "should we buy X or build it", "compare these three observability vendors", "assess Z as our container anchor", "assess X as our anchor", "how locked in are we", "how locked in are we on X", "how exposed are we", "the Y renewal is coming up, prep me", "is this vendor's resilience claim real", "how concentrated are we on Z".

## 14. parvis-risk-regulatory · risk, audit, regulatory and cyber
Your lens on everything examiners, internal audit and the second line inspect in a regulated organization, using the frameworks, regulators and rating scale recorded in the owner skill. An issues ledger mirrors every finding by ID from open to validated closure, with immutable baseline dates and your own paraphrase, never regulator wording. A register holds operational, technology, cyber, third-party and AI risk with acceptances that must carry an expiry, beside the exam and audit calendar and the AI agent inventory with its controls and attestations. Modes cover the risk pulse, exam and audit prep with evidence indexes, remediation review against what validators test, risk acceptance, cyber posture at the altitude of controls, and the "prove our agents are safe" evidence narrative. The org's system of record stays authoritative. Confidential, never synced.
**Invoke.** "log this audit finding", "we got an MRA on X", "run my risk pulse", "prep me for the exam", "prep me for the exam on resilience", "internal audit is coming for DR", "will this remediation pass validation", "draft the risk acceptance", "cyber posture for the board", "prep the tabletop", "add this agent to the inventory", "prove our agents are safe".

## 15. parvis-incident-command · the sev-1 executive layer
What you owe upward, outward and afterward during major incidents. A comms cadence with a kept clock, fill-in templates at T+15, T+60 and resolution per audience track, timestamped incident logs, the regulator-notification consideration checklist, decision support under pressure (the system's one speed-over-depth inversion), executive readouts, blameless post-incident review panels, and drill mode. Never technical troubleshooting, your engineers own diagnosis.
**Invoke.** "we have a sev-1", "major incident in progress", "draft the exec update", "the incident is resolved, help with the readout", "run the post-incident review", "run a tabletop".

## 16. be-human · prose hygiene
The catalog of machine-writing tells and their fixes, plus the punctuation preferences stated in the owner skill. With none stated, the default is no em dashes and colons and semicolons kept rare. Applies to every draft on its own. A bundled copy inside exec-writer keeps documents clean when be-human is absent, and where the two differ, be-human wins.
**Invoke.** Fires on its own for any prose, or "make this sound less like AI", "clean this up".
